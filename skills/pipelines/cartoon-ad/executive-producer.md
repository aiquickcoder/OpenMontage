# Executive Producer - Cartoon Ad Pipeline

## When To Use

Use `cartoon-ad` when the deliverable is a short **generated** cartoon
(Pixar-style 3D by default) with a **native brand / product integration**:
"мультфильм с рекламой", "animated ad", "cartoon with product placement",
"мульт для Reels/Shorts с интеграцией бренда".

Route elsewhere when:
- Rigged, deterministic, locally rendered characters → `character-animation`.
- Motion graphics with no acting → `animation`.
- A presenter talking to camera → `avatar-spokesperson`.

## Contract

- Format: vertical 9:16, 30-90 s, 4-11 shots of at most 8 s each.
- Generation runs **only** through provider `google_flow` (Google AI Pro
  subscription via gflow-cli): `flow_image` (Nano Banana Pro / Nano Banana 2)
  and `flow_veo_video` (Veo 3.1 with native audio). When routing through
  `image_selector` / `video_selector`, always pass
  `allowed_providers: ["google_flow"]`. Never substitute a paid API provider
  (Veo API, fal, Kling, ...) silently — if Flow is unavailable, stop and ask.
- Spoken language: Russian, generated natively by Veo 3.1 inside each clip.
- Text that must be exact (logo, product name, CTA, «Реклама», legal line) is
  **composited** in compose — never generated by Veo or Nano Banana.

## Stage Order

1. `idea` - advertiser brief, product photos, logo, legal constraints.
2. `proposal` - 3+ story concepts with native integration, credit estimate, runtime choice.
3. `script` - shot-by-shot script with exact Russian lines.
4. `character_design` - character bible: style lock, turnarounds, voices, product sheet.
5. `scene_plan` - storyboard: one approved keyframe + assembled Veo prompt per shot.
6. `assets` - image-to-video animation, take review, music.
7. `edit` - cut list, trims, subtitles, end card plan.
8. `compose` - render, loudness, upscale to 1080x1920, QA.
9. `publish` - exports, ad marking, metadata.

## Quality Strategy (why the order matters)

1. **Consistency is decided before any video is generated.** The character
   bible and storyboard keyframes are images, and images in Flow cost no
   credits (daily quota only). Iterate there until every keyframe is right.
2. **Video is always image-to-video from an approved keyframe.** Text-to-video
   for a character shot is a send-back: it re-rolls the character design.
3. **Every prompt is assembled from verbatim bible strings** (style lock,
   character `prompt_tokens`, voice descriptor, product description). Never
   paraphrase them shot to shot — paraphrase is the main source of drift.
4. **Draft cheap, finish expensive.** `veo-fast` (20 credits) for normal shots,
   `veo-quality` (100 credits) only for the hero moment and the product shot,
   after the fast take proved the prompt.

## Credit Governance

- Google AI Pro ≈ 1000 Flow credits / month. Typical 60 s film:
  8 shots × veo-fast × ~1.5 takes ≈ 240 + 2 × veo-quality ≈ 200 → **~450 credits**.
- Read the balance (`gflow credits user`, or `flow_veo_video` dry run) at
  proposal and again before `assets`. If the plan exceeds the remaining
  balance or `FLOW_CREDIT_BUDGET_PER_PROJECT`, shrink the plan with the user.
- Announce every paid generation before running it (shot id, model, credits).
- `count` > 1 multiplies cost — use it only when explicitly planned.
- Anti-bot pacing is enforced in the Flow client (`FLOW_MIN_INTERVAL_SEC`).
  Never run Flow generations in parallel. On a WAF 403 (exit 10) stop for
  30-60 minutes; do not hammer retries.

## Governance Rules

- Run the registry preflight (`provider_menu_summary()`) and confirm
  `google_flow` is available before proposal.
- If both Remotion and HyperFrames are available, present both before locking
  `render_runtime` (AGENT_GUIDE rule).
- Human approval gates: idea, proposal, script, character_design, scene_plan,
  assets, publish. The manifest is binding.
- Every stage runs `meta/reviewer` against the manifest `review_focus`.

## Send-Back Triggers

- Product is a pause in the story rather than the thing that resolves it.
- Product on screen > 25 % of runtime, or claims not backed by the brief.
- A dialogue line does not fit its shot duration.
- A keyframe deviates from the bible (face, outfit, palette, product shape).
- Any character shot generated text-to-video.
- Voice of the same character audibly changes between shots and was not caught.
- Logo / CTA text was generated instead of composited.
- Missing «Реклама» marking.
